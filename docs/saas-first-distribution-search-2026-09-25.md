## Canonical search requirements

- Software must deliver the core recurring customer outcome. Exclude audit/consulting/outsourced operations as the required initial paid product followed by a speculative future SaaS transition.
- Customer interviews, finite onboarding help and occasional exceptions are compatible with SaaS. The test is recurring marginal human labor at 10/100/1,000 customers, not whether any human is involved.
- Prefer self-serve B2B/prosumer/small-team/developer software and qualifying consumer subscriptions. Do not force every opportunity into high-ticket enterprise workflows.
- Do not assume existing audience, enterprise introductions, specialist domain expertise, proprietary datasets or easy data access from AI-assisted coding capability.
- Marketing accessibility means a verified audience/intent surface, an understandable useful outcome, low-friction first value and a credible repeatable distribution path. No promises of effortless marketing or virality.
- Identify actual acquisition surfaces and initial messages, setup burdens, paths from customers 1/10/100/1,000, and both cash and founder-labor costs. Outbound is allowed for learning; permanent bespoke sales as the only growth path is a poor fit.
- The first independent user must receive meaningful value without an already-liquid marketplace or large unrelated user population.
- Balance creation, revenue expansion, convenience, quality, collaboration and new behavior against painful workflow needs. Avoid another list dominated by compliance/document/integration consulting.
- Meaningful novelty concerns customer outcome, segment, delivery economics and distribution. Competitor existence is not automatic rejection; lack of competitors is not automatic validation.
- Verify specialist competitors, plugins, native features and implementation documentation. Vendor-neutral/semantic/AI wording is not a moat.
- Separate file comparison from system replay, a mockup from a working prototype and generic AI output from professional judgment. Test the hardest technical assumption before platform development.
- Natural recurring work, not prizes, badges or notifications, must explain retention.
- Model inference/execution/storage/API/support costs and hidden founder labor; do not invent LTV or assume SaaS margins.
- The $100M+ annual company-revenue ambition is a structural ceiling test, not a forecast. Show paying population, ARPU, penetration and coherent expansion through shared buyers/workflows/data/distribution.
- Explain how defensibility could develop without requiring an already-complete moat. Client-specific history is not automatically a reusable cross-customer data advantage.
- Do not browse/cite DCInside or anonymous community/forum posts. Prefer primary documentation, attributable customer evidence and methodology-bearing research.
- Do not require fixed 70–80% rejection, literal 10x, a guaranteed winner, or precise numerical success scores.
- Separate REJECT CURRENT DESIGN, PARK—RESOURCE MISMATCH, INVESTIGATE and PROTOTYPE TEST. Missing evidence is not disproof.
- Validate the intended software experience, not a substitute consulting engagement. Small functional prototypes are allowed when necessary; no default zero-code rule or forced 72-hour enterprise sale.
- Return at most three finalists, with direct comparative evidence, minimal architecture, hardest feasibility test, distribution mechanism and a bounded experiment. Do not adopt a 1–2-year business from desk research alone.

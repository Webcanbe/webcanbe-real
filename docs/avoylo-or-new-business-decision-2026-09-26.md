## Decision

Recommend choosing Avoylo as the next working business direction and stopping the open-ended new-idea search for now. Do not choose Demand-to-Class as the primary project. This is a real focus recommendation, not a claim that a logistics business is already validated or launch-ready. Operational and spending decisions remain separate from the direction decision.

Reasons:
- The user's explicit objective weights the possibility of a large global infrastructure/network business more highly than the lowest-risk small software product. The earlier SaaS-only preference has been relaxed by the user's explicit willingness to consider Avoylo.
- The user already understands and previously designed Avoylo's box-based seller/platform/host workflow. This lowers rediscovery cost, but is not proof of logistics delivery capability or an established network.
- The recently proposed SaaS alternatives have not established a strong customer-choice advantage or a large accessible paying population either. A hypothetical better idea is not a verified available alternative; indefinite ideation also consumes time.
- A tightly scoped, asset-light entry can be specified without first owning inventory, leasing warehouses, subsidizing a national network or securing a strategic carrier agreement. Whether a particular pilot actually fits the budget still requires its real costs and commitments.

This is a qualitative allocation judgment, not a numerical expected-value or success-probability calculation. The user's 'last attempt' framing is not evidence of a higher success probability and does not justify unlimited financial exposure. Do not imply logistics has a higher attainable value or success probability than SaaS as a category.

## Selected operating shape

- One U.S. region, one shipping-experienced host, initially one merchant with a specific recurring parcel need.
- Merchant-owned, customer-ready sealed boxes; initial host work is receipt/scan, secure storage, locating the correct box, labeling and carrier handoff.
- No arbitrary SKU picking, opening/repacking, broad returns processing, custom delivery fleet, nationwide matching marketplace or compulsory app rebuild in the first shape.
- The label-only interpretation is a proposed/current working scope supported by a historical user description of the host finding a box and attaching a label. Do not claim every older BOX ONLY document defined ready-to-consumer packaging equally precisely.
- A single merchant/host pair can provide a first paid service; a dense network is not a logical prerequisite for first-customer utility. Density could matter later for cost and coverage, but is not an automatic moat.
- Site location should follow an actual merchant's delivery geography and a viable host, not an arbitrarily chosen prestigious city.

## Resource discipline and corrections

User-stated resources: approximately KRW 200,000 and ChatGPT Pro.

Do not assert a universal minimum startup cost without quotes. The previous categorical claim that KRW 200,000 cannot support any Avoylo launch/pilot was too broad. An initial arrangement with no lease, no purchased inventory and merchant-funded actual freight may be affordable; none of those agreements has been obtained.

The budget is not sufficient evidence that a reliable national fulfillment service can be built. Founder cash spend and liability for entrusted inventory are different. A pilot must be sized to its inventory value, actual responsibilities and recovery plan, not just its website cost.

Recommended early cash cap remains KRW 50,000 for initial setup/recruitment/cost discovery, with the remaining funds reserved. This is a recommendation, not money spent or permission to incur obligations. Do not pay a host's recurring subsidy or a merchant's freight merely to manufacture adoption.

The first operating deliverable should be one concrete merchant/host service proposal and all-in cost comparison, not another 30-idea report or a full marketplace. It should include inbound positioning, merchant prepacking labor, storage, host handling, pickup/drop-off, outbound postage, payment/support and loss/recovery exposure. Saving host packing labor may simply shift that labor to the merchant.

If the economics require a subsidy, unavailable carrier discount or uncommitted operator, the first configuration must not launch merely because the business direction was selected. Do not automatically interpret a failed first configuration as proof that every logistics business fails; equally, do not continue open-ended redesign without an affordable route.
